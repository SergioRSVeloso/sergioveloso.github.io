Research

Research themes

My work connects materials physics with biology and chemistry, from the self-assembly of soft gels to how drugs and light-generated electrons move through them.

Theme 01

Soft matter for drug delivery & theranostics

Magnetic & plasmonic lipogels

Injectable gels combining self-assembling dehydropeptides with lipid vesicles and magnetic or plasmonic nanoparticles, enabling release triggered by alternating magnetic fields or 808 nm near-infrared light.

MagnetiteMn ferritesGold nanorodsSilver nanoprisms

Peptide & polyelectrolyte gels

Dehydrodipeptide and RGD hydrogels, chitosan–alginate and chitosan–TPP gels as responsive matrices for anticancer drugs.

Self-assemblypH responseHyperthermia

Hybrid nanocarriers

Lipid, polymeric, protein and mesoporous-silica carriers, including PLGA–PEG, hybrid polymer–lipid and chitosan systems developed within the EU HORIZON project IN-ARMOR.

PLGA–PEGChitosanMesoporous silica

Biological models & devices

Evaluation in 2D cell cultures and 3D tumour spheroids, and integration of nanocarriers into drug-eluting contact lenses.

3D spheroidsContact lenses
Theme 02

Photocatalytic hydrogel microcapsules

Fe³⁺-crosslinked cellulose capsules

Carboxymethyl-cellulose microcapsules, including molecularly imprinted and metal-loaded variants, acting as confined reactors for enzymes and nanoparticles.

Light-driven redox chemistry

Coupling capsules with Photosystem I and flavins to drive photochemical reductions, such as converting nitrate and nitrite into ammonia, with careful quantification of photon flux and catalytic efficiency.

Theme 03

Mathematical modelling of drug release

Reaction–diffusion models

Analytical solutions for release from slabs and spheres that couple diffusion with reaction or binding, using eigenfunction expansions, Laplace transforms and asymptotic analysis.

Bridging theory and experiment

A single dimensionless parameter, α = kL²/D, organises release regimes and explains release exponents below 0.5, connecting physical models to empirical Weibull, Korsmeyer–Peppas and Higuchi fits.
